The story

When Madrid seemed about to fall in November 1936, the government of Francisco Largo Caballero left for Valencia. The city was the capital of the Republic until the government moved on to Barcelona in autumn 1937. This 15th-century Borja palace became the headquarters of the Presidency of the Republican government. The Cortes also sat in Valencia during this period, sharing the Town Hall. Here the Republic reorganised its army and dealt with the crisis of May 1937, when Juan Negrín replaced Largo Caballero. Valencia also hosted the Second International Congress of Writers for the Defence of Culture in July 1937. Since the 1980s the palace has been the seat of the Valencian parliament, the Corts Valencianes.

What you can see today

A Gothic–Renaissance palace facing Plaza de San Lorenzo. There is no Civil War display; the building itself is the point of interest.

Plan your visit

View from the square, a few minutes' walk from the Cathedral. The Corts run booked guided visits on Monday and Friday mornings (not in August); check their website.
