The story

Madrid's first skyscraper (1929) was the tallest building in the city and the Republic's eye over the front: from its upper floors observers watched Nationalist movements in the Casa de Campo. It housed the telephone exchange and the Foreign Press Office, where correspondents such as Hemingway and Dos Passos filed censored reports. That made it a target: Nationalist guns on the Garabitas hill hit it again and again (over 120 impacts in October 1936 alone, according to the company's history), but the exchange never stopped working. The stretch of Gran Vía outside became known as 'Shell Alley'.

What you can see today

The restored tower dominates Gran Vía; the Espacio Fundación Telefónica inside hosts free exhibitions.
