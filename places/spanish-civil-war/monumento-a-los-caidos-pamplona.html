The story

Pamplona was the base of Gen. Emilio Mola, 'the Director' of the July 1936 coup, and Navarre sent thousands of Carlist requetés to the Nationalist side, while more than 3,000 Navarrese left-wingers were killed in the rearguard. The domed Monument to the Fallen of Navarre (Yárnoz and Eusa, 1942) honoured the Nationalist dead; from 1961 its crypt held Mola and Sanjurjo. Their remains were exhumed in 2016 and the square renamed Plaza de la Libertad. The city now plans to turn it into an interpretation centre for democratic memory, while some groups call for demolition — one of Spain's liveliest 'uncomfortable heritage' debates.

What you can see today

The massive domed building dominating the end of Avenida Carlos III, with Francoist inscriptions covered; interior murals of the 'crusade' in the dome.

Plan your visit

Exterior any time. Interior opens only for occasional exhibitions while the transformation project is under way — check the Ayuntamiento de Pamplona website.
