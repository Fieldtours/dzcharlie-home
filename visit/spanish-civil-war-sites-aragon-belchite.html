Spanish Civil War 1936–1939

Spanish Civil War sites in Aragon

Aragon was a front line from the first weeks of the war. Militias dug in along the sierras of the Monegros, where George Orwell served in the first months of 1937, and in August and September 1937 the Republic attacked Belchite, leaving the old town in ruins.

In the winter of 1937–38 the battle of Teruel was fought in bitter cold, and from March 1938 the Nationalist offensive swept through Aragon, reaching the Mediterranean in April. Below are the places from our free Spanish Civil War guide in Aragon, told fairly from both sides.

Aragon

Alcañiz air-raid shelter

Air-raid shelter · 3 Mar 1938 · allow 30 min

Alcañiz council built this shelter in 1937 for about 230 residents of the Almudines quarter.

Aldehuela bunker

Trenches & fortifications · 1937–1938 · allow 30 min

Before the Battle of Teruel the Popular Army built a fortified line south of the city facing Nationalist positions such as the Muela de Villaspesa.

Alto de la Torana bunker (Corbalán)

Trenches & fortifications · 1936–Feb 1938 · allow 45 min

East of Teruel the Republicans built a fortified line on the heights around Corbalán after the militia columns reached the area in summer 1936. Alto de la Torana was its strongest point.

Visiting info checked

Barbastro – Claretian Martyrs Museum

Repression & memory site · Jul–Aug 1936 · allow 45 min

Barbastro stayed in Republican hands after the rising. In the first weeks of the war anarchist militias carried out one of the worst killings of clergy in the Republican zone here.

Basílica del Pilar – the unexploded bombs (Zaragoza)

City site · 3 Aug 1936 · allow 30 min

Early in the war a Republican aircraft attacked Nationalist-held Zaragoza and dropped bombs on the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar, Spain's great Marian shrine.

Battle of the Alfambra interpretation centre (Villarquemado)

Interpretation centre · 5–8 Feb 1938 · allow 1 h

In early February 1938 Franco's army broke the Republican front north of Teruel in the Alfambra valley. It overran the area of Celadas, Alfambra, Villarquemado and Camañas and made thousands of prisoners.

Belchite old town (Pueblo Viejo)

Ruined village · 24 Aug–6 Sep 1937 (retaken by Nationalists 10 Mar 1938) · allow 1 h 30 min

During the Republican Zaragoza offensive the Army of the East under General Sebastián Pozas, with the XI and XV International Brigades, turned on Belchite when the drive on Zaragoza stalled.

Booking neededVisiting info checked

Bielsa museum – the Bielsa Pocket

Museum · Apr–16 Jun 1938 · allow 45 min

When the Nationalist Aragon offensive broke the front in spring 1938, the Republican 43rd Division under Antonio Beltrán ('El Esquinazau') was cut off in the high Pyrenean valley of Bielsa.

Visiting info checked

Caspe – battle of March 1938

City site · 15–17 Mar 1938 (Council of Aragon 1936–37) · allow 45 min

From 1936 Caspe was the seat of the anarchist-led Regional Defence Council of Aragon, which ran the collectivised Republican part of the region.

Civil War in Aragon interpretation centre (Robres)

Interpretation centre · 1936–1938 · allow 1 h

Robres lay just behind the Republican side of the Sierra de Alcubierre front, which was held from 1936 to 1938 by anarchist (CNT-FAI) and POUM militia columns and later by the Popular Army.

Codo – battle of the Montserrat requetés

Battlefield · 24–25 Aug 1937 · allow 30 min

At the start of the Zaragoza offensive the Republican 25th Division attacked the small village of Codo, between Belchite and Quinto.

El Muletón (Tortajada)

Battlefield · Dec 1937–Jan 1938 · allow 1 h

El Muletón is a steep hill north-west of Teruel that commands the road north and the Alfambra valley. The Nationalists fortified it as part of the city's outer defences, with trenches, shelters and a concrete cistern.

Estrecho Quinto bunker (Huesca front)

Trenches & fortifications · late 1936–1938 · allow 30 min

After July 1936, Nationalist-held Huesca was almost surrounded by Republican columns, and the siege lines around the city lasted until March 1938.

Fuentes de Ebro – tank attack of October 1937

Battlefield · 13 Oct 1937 · allow 20 min

As the last act of the Zaragoza offensive, the Republicans attacked the fortified town of Fuentes de Ebro, south-east of Zaragoza.

Las Viñas bunker (Teruel)

Trenches & fortifications · 1936–1938 · allow 20 min

The Las Viñas sector around Teruel cemetery was contested from the very start of the war. A defence line on the ridge above the prison watched the Turia riverbed and the old Zaragoza road below the city.

Mansueto fort (Valdecebro)

Trenches & fortifications · 1937–1938 · allow 30 min

Mansueto is a promontory guarding the south-eastern approach to Teruel above Valdecebro.

Montearagón castle (Huesca siege lines)

Trenches & fortifications · 1936–1938 · allow 1 h

The 11th-century castle-abbey of Montearagón stands on a hill east of Huesca. From 1936 to 1938 it was part of the Republican ring around Nationalist-held Huesca, together with the Estrecho Quinto bunker below.

Muela de Villaspesa

Trenches & fortifications · 1937–1938 · allow 45 min

The Muela de Villaspesa was an intermediate fortified point south of Teruel overlooking the Republican positions at Aldehuela and the Turia valley.

Pozos de Caudé memorial (Concud)

Repression & memory site · Jul 1936–Dec 1937 · allow 30 min

The site is a well about 50 m deep beside an old inn on the Teruel–Zaragoza road, near Concud.

Quinto – El Piquete church

City site · 25–26 Aug 1937 · allow 30 min

The old parish church of the Assumption, called 'El Piquete', stands on the Cerro de la Corona above Quinto. In August 1937 about 300 Nationalist defenders turned it into a fortress, and civilians also took refuge inside.

Booking neededVisiting info checked

Quinto – Purburel / Cabezo de la Nariz trenches

Trenches & fortifications · 24–26 Aug 1937 · allow 1 h 15 min

Quinto was a fortified Nationalist outpost on the Ebro that had been on the front for a year. Its main strongpoint was on the Purburel and Cabezo de la Nariz hills, held by about 650 men behind wire, trenches and concrete works.

Visiting info checked

Ruta Orwell – Monte Irazo & Las Tres Huegas (Sierra de Alcubierre)

Trenches & fortifications · Jan–16 Feb 1937 (front static Aug 1936–Mar 1938) · allow 2 h

From late 1936 the Aragon front ran across the bare Sierra de Alcubierre. Anarchist and POUM militia columns faced Nationalist positions such as San Simón, and the front hardly moved for more than a year.

Torrero Cemetery memorials (Zaragoza)

Repression & memory site · 1936–1946; memorial 1980 · allow 45 min

Zaragoza fell to the military rising on 19 July 1936 without a fight and became the Nationalists' main base on the Aragon front.
