Spanish Civil War 1936–1939

Battle of the Ebro & Catalonia sites

The Battle of the Ebro was the longest battle of the Spanish Civil War. On 25 July 1938 the Republican army crossed the river and advanced into the Terra Alta, but it was stopped at Gandesa and pushed back across the Ebro by mid-November after months of fighting in the hills.

Catalonia also keeps the memory of the bombing of Barcelona, with its shelters and anti-aircraft batteries, and of the exile of early 1939, when hundreds of thousands crossed into France. Questions

How many places are there to visit?

This page lists 29 places from the free The Spanish Civil War 1936–1939 guide, each with its story, what you can see today, how to visit and GPS directions.

How much time do I need?

Allowing the suggested time at each place adds up to about 25 hours on site, plus driving between them. Plan on about 5 days to see everything, or pick the places that matter most to you. The guide's tour planner puts any selection in driving order.

Do I need to book?

Most places can be visited without booking. 7 of them need booking or can only be seen on a guided visit; they are marked “Booking needed” below. For 17 places we checked opening times and prices on official websites in September 2026; open each place to see them. Opening times change, so check before you travel.

Can I visit every site?

Some places, especially open fields, are on private land: view them from the road or a public path. Cemeteries and memorials are places of remembrance first.

Is the guide free, and does it work without signal?

Yes. The The Spanish Civil War 1936–1939 guide is free and works on your phone with no signal once it has been opened, with maps, stories and a tour planner. There is nothing to install from an app store.
